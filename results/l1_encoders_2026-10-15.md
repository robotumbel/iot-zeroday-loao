# L1 transformer encoders vs RF — P1/P3, family level (2026-10-15)

Setup: NF-ToN-IoT-v3 and NF-BoT-IoT-v3 (cap 20,000 per raw label, de-duplicated, 47 NetFlow v3 features), family-level splits, 10 × P1 + 4 × P3 = 14 splits, seed 0. One seed and two datasets, so these are pilot results, not paper results.

Note (later the same day): these seed-0 files were moved to `results/archive_pre_maha/` when the 3-seed run with the Mahalanobis score started; the paths below are relative to that folder.

Tables: `results/compare_l1_far1_c0_family_seed0.md` (1% budget, clean warm-up), `..._far1_c0.05_...` (5% contaminated warm-up), `..._far5_c0_...` (5% budget). Raw rows: `results/l1_*_family_seed0.csv`, `results/rf_msp_*_family_seed0.csv`. Per-flow scores for every L1 split: `results/scores/L1-*/` (thresholds can be recomputed without retraining).

## Methods

| Method | Details |
|---|---|
| RF-MSP (full train) | Random Forest, 100 trees, all training flows |
| RF-MSP (2k/unit) | same, but the identical 2,000-flows-per-unit training sample the encoders use |
| L1 tiny-local | 2-layer BERT (hidden 128) trained from scratch, WordPiece vocabulary built on training texts — "no pretraining" ablation |
| L1 DistilBERT | `distilbert/distilbert-base-uncased`, fine-tuned 2 epochs, lr 5e-5, batch 32, fp16 |
| L1 SecBERT | `jackaduma/SecBERT`, same recipe |

Text input: `sentence` serialization (e.g. "in bytes is high, protocol is 6, ..."), about 430 tokens for DistilBERT and 340 for SecBERT, nothing truncated at 512. Open-set scores: MSP, energy, and kNN (cosine distance to the 10th nearest of 5,000 training embeddings, mean-pooled last layer). Thresholds come from an unlabeled 2,000-flow warm-up window from the target network (`target_warmup_far`), clean (0%) or 5% contaminated.

## Results — AUROC-OOD (threshold-free)

| Method (best score) | P1 mean | P3 mean |
|---|---|---|
| RF-MSP (full train) | **0.881** | **0.575** |
| RF-MSP (2k/unit) | **0.886** | 0.530 |
| DistilBERT (kNN) | 0.836 | 0.513 |
| SecBERT (kNN) | 0.831 | 0.529 |
| tiny-local (kNN) | 0.817 | 0.495 |

For every encoder, kNN on embeddings beats energy and MSP; MSP from the classifier head is the weakest score and falls below 0.4 in P3.

Per split, encoders win in two P3 cases: BoT → ToN FLOOD (DistilBERT kNN 0.819 vs RF 0.51–0.63) and ToN → BoT FLOOD (SecBERT kNN 0.933 vs RF 0.89). They lose clearly on ToN BRUTEFORCE (P1), ToN FLOOD (P1) and BoT → ToN RECON (P3).

**ToN → BoT RECON is inverted for every method** (AUROC 0.03–0.25, encoders included). Since an RF and three different transformers all fail the same way, this is a property of the data (ToN benign/scanning collisions), not of a model. It must be reported and discussed, probably with a de-duplicated-by-class or relabeled variant of ToN.

## Results — at a fixed false-alarm budget (warm-up calibrated, clean window)

| Method (kNN for encoders) | P1 unknown recall @1% | P3 unknown recall @1% | P1 @5% | P3 @5% | P3 benign → known attack |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RF full | **0.375** | 0.085 | **0.638** | 0.128 | 0.186 |
| RF 2k | 0.324 | 0.005 | 0.634 | 0.086 | 0.217 |
| DistilBERT | 0.051 | **0.116** | 0.424 | **0.134** | 0.316 |
| SecBERT | 0.060 | 0.046 | 0.267 | 0.104 | 0.489 |
| tiny-local | 0.057 | 0.012 | 0.244 | 0.033 | 0.318 |

- The 1% budget holds for every method (benign rejected as unknown 0.4–1.0%).
- In P1, RF's scores are far more useful in the extreme tail: at 1% the encoders catch only about 5% of zero-day flows. Their AUROC is decent but their unknown scores overlap benign traffic in the top percentile.
- In P3, DistilBERT-kNN gives the best recall at both budgets, but all methods are weak (≤ 13%).
- Encoders confuse target benign traffic with known attacks more often than RF (32–49% vs 19–22%).
- With a 5%-contaminated warm-up, recall drops for most methods (RF full P1 0.375 → 0.308; DistilBERT P3 0.116 → 0.067): zero-day flows inside the warm-up window raise the threshold.

|---|---|---|

| SecBERT | 3.0–3.1 ms/flow | 2–5 min |

## What this means for the paper

1. **Honest pilot finding: fine-tuned encoders on serialized NetFlow do not beat a Random Forest for zero-day detection**, neither within a dataset nor across datasets, at a much higher inference cost (GPU, 3–4 ms/flow). This is publishable as a benchmark finding, but the paper's story should not depend on "LLMs win".
2. **Where encoders help:** cross-network FLOOD detection (two of four P3 splits), and pretraining adds a small but consistent gain over the no-pretraining ablation (kNN AUROC +0.02 in P1, +0.02–0.03 in P3). The security-domain SecBERT is not better than general DistilBERT.
3. **The score matters more than the model:** kNN on embeddings > energy > MSP in every case. Next methods should keep distance-based scoring (and try Mahalanobis, and kNN on RF leaf embeddings for a fair non-LLM counterpart).
4. **Two dataset-level issues dominate the numbers** and are themselves contributions: ToN benign/scanning collisions (inverted RECON) and zero-day contamination of warm-up windows.

## Next steps

- Repeat with 3–5 seeds and the subfamily level before drawing conclusions; add confidence intervals and a Wilcoxon test.
- Try L1 variants that address the tail problem: contrastive/metric fine-tuning (SiamXBERT-style), Mahalanobis score, more epochs, `binned` vs `sentence` serialization (RQ4).
- Add a non-LLM deep baseline (MLP or FT-Transformer with the same kNN/energy scores) to separate "transformer" from "language pretraining".
- Time RF inference for the cost table.
